### Notes

1. ELDA Lead Systems develops leadership effectiveness within complex systems through experiential learning conducted in challenging environments.
2. The selected ELDA activity provides the experiential vehicle for leadership development; completion of the activity is not, in itself, the primary learning outcome.
3. Course activities are to expose learners to interdependencies, competing priorities, incomplete information, resource constraints and relationships beyond direct authority, providing opportunities to understand and influence a wider system.
4. Psychometric tools, self-evaluation, observation and feedback may be used to increase learner awareness of how their leadership preferences and behaviours affect relationships, influence and outcomes within the wider system.
5. Learners are to be provided opportunities to deliberately apply influence and adaptive leadership behaviours where outcomes cannot be achieved through direct authority alone.
6. Army ethos and values are to be demonstrated through leadership behaviour and judgement, particularly when balancing competing demands, relationships and organisational outcomes.
7. Learning identified during ELDA Lead Systems is to inform continued reflection, behavioural adaptation and deliberate leadership development beyond the course.
8. The Learning Provider is the New Zealand Army Leadership Centre (NZALC).
9. Instructor allocation and activity selection remain the responsibility of NZALC and are to comply with the NZALC Safety Management Plan and applicable activity SOPs.

## Course Structure

### COG 1: Systems Leadership and Influence

#### LO 1.1: Demonstrate Army ethos, values and sound judgement within complex environments

Learners demonstrate Army ethos and values through their leadership behaviour and judgement while operating within complex environments characterised by competing demands, uncertainty, interdependence and pressure.

#### LO 1.2: Analyse relationships and interdependencies within a complex system

Learners identify and analyse key stakeholders, relationships, dependencies, competing perspectives, constraints and potential consequences within a complex system in order to better understand how leadership actions may affect wider outcomes.

#### LO 1.3: Influence others to achieve outcomes beyond direct authority

Learners deliberately apply communication, relationship-building and influence behaviours to achieve outcomes through individuals and groups over whom they may not exercise direct authority.

#### LO 1.4: Evaluate and adapt leadership behaviour to improve system effectiveness

Learners evaluate the impact of their leadership behaviour on relationships, influence and wider system outcomes, and deliberately adapt their behaviour to improve leadership effectiveness across complex contexts.
